name: migrate
description: "In TurboOffice, a whole deal coming over from another system with its paper, live or already over — \"here is the Dotloop folder for 12 Oak St, bring it over\""
---

A migration is a deal arriving with its paper from another system: a Dotloop loop, another platform's file, a folder of PDFs. Gather the whole pile before judging any of it: every file handed over for this deal, each by its file handle, and ask for more when the pile points at paper it does not hold. Then sort it, saying what each document is: the representation agreement, the operative contract, its amendments, a termination or a withdrawal, offers that died and counters a later one replaced, duplicates, supporting paper, and paper that belongs to another deal. Read pages (`read_file`) only as far as it takes to say. From the sorted paper decide whether the deal is over (closed, terminated, withdrawn, expired) or still in play.

Before committing anything, tell the person the plan: the deal it goes on (one of theirs, found by its address and clients, or a new one), the paper it commits and in what order, and what the deal will still owe once it is on (its needs: the agreement, the disclosures, the contract, a settlement statement once it has closed), naming the needs this pile cannot meet as the question back to them. Where the paper and what the person says disagree, ask; it is not yours to settle. Commit only after their yes.

Each commit goes through a door that already exists, as an approval card where the door stages one. Route the next paper once the card before it is approved: the router reads each file against the deal as it then stands. A sale that closed is recorded with `record_closed_deal`, its price, closing day and commission as the plan stated them from the paper (one the paper does not state is asked for), with the files it is recorded from; its other paper is filed with `file_to_deal`. A sale on a deal the person already has is that deal's close (`act_on_deal` `close`), which `record_closed_deal` names when it refuses. A live deal is the one chosen with `use_deal`, or, when none of theirs is it, the deal `route_document` proposes from its engagement with no deal chosen. Then `route_document`, in the order the paper was signed: the engagement, the operative contract as one packet (the offer with the counters and addenda that make it the contract), each executed amendment, and a termination or release that ended a contract. Everything else is filed with `file_to_deal`, `documentType` saying what it is. An offer that died or a counter a later one replaced is filed, never routed: it does nothing to the deal. A card whose reading differs from the plan is the stop: say how, and the person's correction goes back with `answer_proposal`. Paper on the wrong deal, the card staged there included, moves to the right deal with `file_to_deal({ documentId })` once that deal is chosen, and is routed there. A deal that ended without a sale (an expired or withdrawn listing, a buyer who stopped looking) has no door of its own: say so, and bring it over only on the person's word, as a live deal, then archived with `act_on_deal` `archive`.

No client is emailed by the paper coming over: routing, filing and a closed sale's record send nothing. A seller or buyer seated with an email on a live deal is that deal's client from then on, and the platform's invitations to clients can follow (a seller to the disclosure on a live listing, clients to the closing on the calendar), so the plan names who it seats.

Its tools: route_document, answer_proposal, file_to_deal, record_closed_deal, act_on_deal.
