# TurboOffice

The tools come from the TurboOffice connector (`https://turbooffice.ai/mcp`). Connect it and sign in first.

Turbo Office is the platform a real estate brokerage and its agents use to run listings and buyer deals from the agreement through closing: the deals, their offers, counteroffers and contracts, the documents on file, and what each deal still needs. This connection acts as one agent of one brokerage and sees what that agent sees in the app. The read tools read. With write access, a few tools change things at once that can be taken back: act_on_task, act_on_appointment, note, act_on_need (marking what a deal has on file or skipping what it no longer needs), and reopen_offer for an offer someone marked not accepted or withdrawn. Each says how it is undone. Others stage a card: a change that waits for the person's approval, with a `commits` line saying what approving it does. A card is approved in Turbo Office, or with approve_card where this connection holds it, and only after the person has seen its `commits` line and said yes in this conversation. Sending paper for signatures is a card too: act_on_envelope with `review_and_send` stages it, naming the paper and who is emailed to sign. work_on hands anything else to Turbo Office's own assistant: drafting and answering paper, starting listings and buyer deals, CMAs, corrections. It works in a thread that is also open in the person's Turbo Office chat, answers within about 25 seconds with the thread and what is done so far, and get_thread reads the rest. It never signs, sends or approves anything: what needs the person comes back as a card, or a draft they open in Turbo Office by its link. Signing is done by the person and their clients in Turbo Office; each deal and offer result carries its address there. Ids are plain database ids: take a deal, offer, file, need, task or thread id from one tool's result and pass it to the next. When the user wants something no tool here does, or a tool refuses and you cannot work around it, tell the team with note_friction. Skills group the tools by kind of work, and load_skill returns a skill's guide: what its tools are for and how to use them well. A skill whose work runs in Turbo Office's own assistant says so in its guide: hand that work to work_on. The skills:
- `offer` — an offer on a property — from our buyer, or written up on the caller's own listing — "offer on 155 Main St, 540k, 10-day DD, close in 30"
- `contract` — an offer already on the chain, ours or theirs, and everything done to it after: a change to its terms whatever the request calls it — "move closing on the Brooks contract to Aug 19", "drop Philip's price 5k and give them until Friday" — pulling our own paper back (discard a draft, withdraw a sent offer, recall it to edit, bring back a withdrawn one, record that the other side signed it, record the contract from a file already on the deal), answering theirs (decline, set aside or accept their counter or amendment, mark the buyers' offer not accepted, record that they rejected or withdrew, undo any of those), ending a bound contract by one party's right or by mutual release — "the buyer is terminating during due diligence" — and fixing what the platform's RECORD of an offer says the paper contains — "the earnest money on Philip's offer is 5k not 500". A new offer with no paper yet is the `offer` skill
- `sign` — a document to send out for signature that is not one of the deal's instruments — "get the attorney's signature on this exhibit", "prep this affidavit to sign"
- `route-document` — a file that is one of a deal's instruments — in this conversation or already on the deal — signed or not
- `cma` — a CMA — pricing a home from the sales around it — "run a CMA on 117 Terrace Dr", "what should we list the Loridans house at?"
- `forms` — a form from the brokerage's library that no other skill composes — a disclosure, an addendum, a touring or agency agreement, the brokerage's own form — the paper a deal still owes (its listing or buyer agreement, the seller's disclosure), and a new buyer client started on their representation agreement — "start a touring agreement for Dana", "draft the listing agreement on Wren Hollow"
- `signers` — who signs and where: add, replace or remove a signer on a draft or on paper out for signatures, add a seller or buyer to a deal, take one off or put one on or off title (a listing or buyer agreement's signers follow the deal), put a draft's signing fields back to the form's own — "add a signer to the listing agreement", "reset the form"
- `deal` — the deal itself or its clients: archive a deal or take it out of the archive, move it between the stages before a contract, change a listing's list price, close a deal (one whose contract was signed outside the app too) or correct a closed deal's price, day or commission, move a contract's closing date, share uploaded files with the clients, correct a seller's or buyer's name, email or phone — "archive the old Kaden deal", "send the inspection to the sellers for their records", "Kaden's email is wrong, it's kaden@gmail.com", "we're live, mark it listed", "drop the list price to 425", "it closed Oct 7 at 262k", and a sale that already closed off the platform, recorded so it counts in the agent's closed numbers — "add my closing on 4 Elm St from March, 412k, 3%"
- `pricing` — a deal's pricing and its agreement's period: move a listing or a buy deal to another pricing plan, adjust its commission or a fee, extend or shorten the listing or engagement period, or draft a new agreement rather than an amendment — "move the Kaden listing to the 2.5% plan", "drop Kaden's commission to 2.75", "extend the listing through March", "the sellers want a new listing agreement, not an amendment"
- `migrate` — a whole deal coming over from another system with its paper, live or already over — "here is the Dotloop folder for 12 Oak St, bring it over"
