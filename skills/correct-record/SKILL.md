description: "In TurboOffice, the platform's RECORD of an offer is wrong — a mis-read price, a wrong date, a term the paper never stated — \"the earnest money on Philip's offer is 5k not 500\", \"closing is the 30th, not the 3rd\""
---

A correction fixes what the record says the paper contains; it does not change what the parties agreed — a new price, an amendment, a counter is a change to the terms (the `change` skill), so say so instead of recording. It needs the offer: the deal's brief (`use_deal`) prints each offer's id as offer id. `read_recorded_terms({ offerId })` lists the terms the record holds for that offer, each with the exact factKey a correction must use and the value in the term's own shape (a dollar amount, a percent, a date, or a period measured from a contract event), plus the plain-English brief the page shows. `correct_record({ offerId, changes, brief? })` stages the corrections as an approval card — nothing is applied until the user approves it on the card. Each change names a term the record ALREADY has (its factKey), `after` in the term's own shape — amount / percent for money; date, or relative (days from a contract event — anchor is an event factKey such as offer.effective_date, negative days = before) for a date; not_stated when the paper does not state the term at all, which retires it — and your verification against the paper: verified means you opened the paper with `read_file` before staging and read the term there (the chain receipt and `list_files` print the ids it takes; the record, its brief and the chain's captured changes are not the paper), so verified_agrees / verified_contradicts cite the excerpt you read as evidence, and unverified says plainly that you did not read it. The human is authoritative over the record: when you hold verified contradicting evidence they have not acknowledged, push back once — quote what you read, ask whether to record anyway, no tool call; if they confirm, record with verified_contradicts. Being unable to verify is never a reason to refuse. `brief` revises the words the page shows (the summary paragraph, the flagged points) — give the full replacement for what changes; use it when a correction makes the brief say something the record no longer does, or when the user asks for different wording alone. When its card is staged, say what will change and how you verified it.

Its tools: read_recorded_terms, correct_record.
