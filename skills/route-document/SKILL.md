---
name: route-document
description: "In TurboOffice, a file that is one of a deal's instruments — in this conversation or already on the deal — signed or not"
---

Files in the conversation are material; the request and what is on them say what they are — a deal's instrument is routed whatever its signatures, and a document with no place in the deal's record that the user wants signed is the `sign` skill. `route_document({ files })` treats the PDFs you name as paper that came in and proposes what they do to the deal — file, bind, apply an amendment, terminate, start signing — resolving the deal from the documents when none is chosen, and stages that proposal. A staged proposal is put in front of the user as an approval card with its own Approve button — that card, not you, commits it. So do not ask "shall I?": answer with a short paragraph in the user's words — what came in (which documents, from whom), whose paper it is and which of the user's deals it belongs to, with what on the page told you so, what you saw in them (what changed against the deal, who signed or initialed what, what is not yet bound), and what approving will record — then stop; the card carries the approve, so it is usually worth pointing at it. Several papers in one request are routed one packet each, and each gets its own card. If the user replies with a correction or a deal choice instead, `answer_proposal` carries it back to the router — never route again to correct a proposal. When the user wants the paper only kept on file ("file it", "documents only, do not bind") on a card that would prepare it for signature or bind the deal, `answer_proposal({ attentionItemId, disposition: "file" })` files it at once and closes the card; nothing else changes. A file the user asks only to keep on the chosen deal, with no card in play, is `file_to_deal`. Paper filed on the wrong deal moves to the right one with `file_to_deal({ documentId })` once that deal is chosen, and is routed there; a card staged on the wrong deal takes no deal pick. Our buyers' signed offer that came in is recorded as our offer, out to the sellers (the card `route_document` stages for it). Getting it "to the sellers" from there is one of two things: forwarding the signed paper to the listing side as it is, which nothing here does (this chat sends no mail), or collecting the sellers' signatures on this same paper here, which `send_for_their_signatures` drafts once the offer is recorded — their signatures stay and nobody on our side signs again. When the words do not say which, ask. When the request is to draft *from* what came in ("respond to this", "our amendment to their proposal"), `read_file({ artifactId })` opens it instead, and the drafting skill takes it from there.

Its tools: route_document, answer_proposal, file_to_deal.

Here you can be the reader. route_document has Turbo Office read the paper; propose_routing takes your own reading instead: read the file (its pages, or read_file) and the deal (get_deal, get_offer_chain, get_needs), and say in `finding` what each document in the file is (segments by page range, form code when the form is in the library, kind, and who signed it: `signedRoles`, the role keys whose lines carry a signature), which is the instrument, how it relates to the deal (new, which an agreement is too, counter, amendment, executed_copy, supersedes, supporting), and the terms it states; executionStatus is your judgment over the instrument's signed roles: unsigned with none, partially_signed with some, fully_executed only when every side that must sign has. A field with nothing to say (anchors, disposition) can be left out. Turbo Office composes and stages the same card either way; a reading that contradicts itself is refused, saying which, and a reading that was wrong is corrected by proposing again. With no deal, `newDeal` names the deal the paper would create, and the card creates it. A folder of a whole deal's paper is one call, not one per document: read every file first, then send the deal's plan to propose_routing as `instruments` (each instrument's files and finding, in the order the paper was signed) and `supporting` (the other files, each with its documentType and title), with dealId or newDeal. Turbo Office stages one card that says what approving does to each piece, and the person approves it once; it runs each instrument through its own card in order, files the rest, and stops at a step that is refused, leaving that step's card open. A migration emails no one: a deal created from paper does not contact its clients until the person turns that on (`newDeal.clientsIntroduced` asks for it), and paper brought onto an existing deal emails its people only if that deal contacts its clients.
