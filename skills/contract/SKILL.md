The offer chain: an offer that exists on it, ours or theirs, and everything done to it after. A new offer with no paper yet is not here: that is the `offer` skill, which drafts it, and an offer reaches the chain when its draft is read at send or when theirs arrives. Three kinds of work live here, each with its own doors, and which one a request is follows from what it asks for, not from the word it uses: a change to the terms (with answering their paper and pulling ours back), ending a bound contract, and correcting what the record says the paper contains. The judgment calls between them are in each part.

## A change to the terms

A change alters the terms of an offer we sent or a contract that is bound. Which paper it takes follows from where the deal stands, not from the word the request uses ("amendment", "counter", "update" all arrive here), and the user's account of where it stands outranks the record's: when they say the other side signed, the record is behind and recording that comes first (below). It needs the deal: choose it with `use_deal` (its brief lists the offers and marks the bound one), then read `get_offer_chain` for the terms the record holds, the paper waiting on an answer, every instrument applied since, and drafts in flight; open an instrument with `read_file({ envelopeId })` when the request turns on wording the term changes don't carry. `start_change` takes the proposal as facts — only what the user asked to change, stated against the terms the chain receipt shows, in the same delta vocabulary an inbound amendment is read into — plus a title and summary; it records the proposal on the chain, pre-assigns the signers and hands the envelope to drafting, which writes the paper from the user's request. Bound contract → the amendment form; an offer not yet bound (sent, countered, or expired unsigned) → the counteroffer/modification form, which needs its own time limit to accept (a literal offer.acceptance_deadline — if the user did not say one, ask); the receipt names which. An offer of ours that expired has a second way back: `withdraw_paper` with `intent: "recall"` brings the offer itself back as an editable draft (its terms carried, its time limit blank, every signature on it reset), so our clients sign the whole offer again, where the counteroffer/modification keeps their signatures on the offer and asks them to sign one more page. Unless the user's words pick one, put both to them. A counteroffer of ours is always the reply to their pending counter. When their amendment is pending on a bound contract, `answers` carries the user's word on it: an amendment that contends for the same terms is the reply to theirs, and one on independent terms can stand alongside it with both live until one executes. Propose the reply when the terms overlap and name the alternative when they do not; an amendment is refused until one of them is chosen.

Either side authors it: on our listing, a change answers the buyer's offer the same way. `start_change` takes any offer that is neither bound nor terminated, one marked rejected, withdrawn or expired included: sending the counteroffer reopens it. A countered offer with nothing pending on either side (their counter declined) takes a new counteroffer the same way. Drafting is free: a change is composed whatever else is in flight, and nothing is sent by starting it. An amendment is a delta on the contract: more than one of ours can be out for signatures at once, and each applies in the order it executes; its send re-reads it against the contract as it stands and refuses only when a term it states would land differently than reviewed. A counteroffer of ours is one at a time. The chain receipt shows what is in flight, and `act_on_envelope`'s `review_and_send` lists what else of ours is out, so say so when you send behind it.

When the user says the other side signed an instrument of ours — the offer, a counter, or an amendment to a bound contract — that the chain shows out for signatures or signed by our side only, the record is behind: `record_execution` with that instrument's id printed as paper id stages the card that records it (with the day they signed when the user said one), and a change they asked for alongside is composed once that card is approved. When the user wants a change and the deal's brief says its contract is not recorded, `route_document` with the file that is the contract comes first: tell the user in plain text that the change is composed once they approve the card. A bound contract needs no recording of its own, and a change to it that nobody has signed yet goes straight to `start_change`. When the user wants different terms on an offer of ours that is not bound — drafted and unsent, or out for signatures — and does not say it was signed, there is no change to compose and it is not edited from here: point the user at that offer's own page and say in one line where the offer stands and that the price (or whichever term) is changed on its page — edit the draft, or recall it back to a draft first when it is out for signatures.

A draft of a change of ours still in the editor is not a second start: continue on it with `work_on`, naming the draft's envelope id — the draft becomes this chat's and the request continues on it, or, when another chat holds it, the user gets a Resume-draft button and you say who holds it. When the user says to send a draft of ours, `act_on_envelope({ envelopeId, action: { intent: "review_and_send" } })` puts its send in front of them with what still stops it; the send itself is theirs, on the paper. A counter or amendment of ours already out for signatures cannot be resumed or edited: say plainly that it is out for signatures, what it holds and who has signed. When the user asks to pull our paper back from here, `withdraw_paper` stages the way back as an approval card; say what the packet holds. Paper the user has called signed or executed in this conversation is not pulled back on their say-so: the two statements contradict each other, and that contradiction is a question to put to them before any card. Which door depends on whose paper it is: our sent OFFER (out for signatures, or signed by our side and never executed) takes `intent: "recall"`, which brings it back as an editable draft; our COUNTER or AMENDMENT out for signatures takes `intent: "void"`, and `start_change` composes the replacement once that card is approved. The same `void` pulls back a counter or amendment of ours that our side has signed and the other side has not: the node is retired, the signed copy stays on file as a record, nobody is emailed, and any paper of theirs it answered waits on our answer again. If the request is already what that packet says, say so and stop.

The other side's paper is decided with `decide_their_paper`: decline what is waiting on our answer (on our listing, the buyers' offer itself is marked not accepted), set aside a proposal the chain receipt shows was made against terms that have since changed, or accept, which drafts the signing packet for our clients. Our offer or counter that our side signed outside the product goes to the other side for their signatures here with `send_for_their_signatures`, which drafts their packet the same way: each of their signers is seated from the name and email the user gives, so ask for them, and when the other side has its own agent, sending to their clients directly is for when that agent has agreed. When the other side answered outside the platform (the sellers rejected our offer outright, the buyers withdrew theirs), `record_their_answer` records it. When a recording of ours was wrong (the amendment was never signed after all) or the executed copy will never come, `record_execution` takes `intent: "retract"` or `"waive_copy"` on the same paper id. Each of these but accept stages a card, as `withdraw_paper` does. Reopening undoes a decision at once and sends nothing: an offer marked rejected or withdrawn, or their counter or amendment that was declined.

## Ending a bound contract

A termination ends a bound contract. It needs that deal's bound offer: the deal's brief (`use_deal`) prints each offer's id as offer id and marks the bound contract, and `get_offer_chain` on it confirms the contract and shows what is in flight. Two instruments, and the user's words decide which: `mutual` when both parties release each other; `unilateral` when one party exercises a right it holds (name `terminatingParty`). A situation — "my buyer wants out", "the seller is walking" — is not yet a choice of instrument: ask which, and whether earnest money was delivered / how it is disbursed when that matters, before starting. Which of the two is the user's choice; a draft of the one they did not mean is paper to discard.

Three doors. `start_termination` is the paper: it mints the envelope with the request on it, and our drafter picks the state's form for the flavor, seats the signers and carries `disbursement` onto the disbursement line the paper carries. Its refusal while a draft is already in flight names that draft by the id printed as envelope id — passing it back as `reviseEnvelopeId` voids that draft and replaces it with this one, which is how a termination's terms are changed before it is signed. `record_termination` is the door for a contract the user says already ended outside the platform: it stages an approval card, and approving it runs the unwind with no instrument and no signing, and no paper on file afterward. A signed release the user brought in is neither: it is paper that came in, and recording it is the route-document skill's work. Composing is the default; take the paperless door only on the user's word that it is already done elsewhere.

## Correcting the record

A correction fixes what the record says the paper contains; it does not change what the parties agreed — a new price, an amendment, a counter is a change to the terms (a change to the terms, above), so say so instead of recording. It needs the offer: the deal's brief (`use_deal`) prints each offer's id as offer id. `read_recorded_terms({ offerId })` lists the terms the record holds for that offer, each with the exact factKey a correction must use and the value in the term's own shape (a dollar amount, a percent, a date, or a period measured from a contract event), plus the plain-English brief the page shows. `correct_record({ offerId, changes, brief? })` stages the corrections as an approval card — nothing is applied until the user approves it on the card. Each change names a term the record ALREADY has (its factKey), `after` in the term's own shape — amount / percent for money; date, or relative (days from a contract event — anchor is an event factKey such as offer.effective_date, negative days = before) for a date; not_stated when the paper does not state the term at all, which retires it — and your verification against the paper: verified means you opened the paper with `read_file` before staging and read the term there (the chain receipt and `list_files` print the ids it takes; the record, its brief and the chain's captured changes are not the paper), so verified_agrees / verified_contradicts cite the excerpt you read as evidence, and unverified says plainly that you did not read it. The human is authoritative over the record: when you hold verified contradicting evidence they have not acknowledged, push back once — quote what you read, ask whether to record anyway, no tool call; if they confirm, record with verified_contradicts. Being unable to verify is never a reason to refuse. `brief` revises the words the page shows (the summary paragraph, the flagged points) — give the full replacement for what changes; use it when a correction makes the brief say something the record no longer does, or when the user asks for different wording alone. When its card is staged, say what will change and how you verified it.

Its tools: start_change, withdraw_paper, record_execution, route_document, decide_their_paper, send_for_their_signatures, record_their_answer, reopen_offer, start_termination, record_termination, read_recorded_terms, correct_record.
